Sexual abuse and rape represent a deeply disturbing reality for many children, particularly girls, in our society. The silence surrounding these crimes creates an environment where perpetrators operate with impunity, and survivors suffer in isolation. INADESU's advocacy work focuses on breaking this silence and building protective mechanisms.

## The Scale of the Problem

In communities across Cameroon, children face alarming risks of sexual violence. Girls are disproportionately affected, often targeted by family members, authority figures, and community members who exploit power imbalances. The trauma inflicted on these young victims extends far beyond the immediate physical harm—it affects their education, mental health, social relationships, and future opportunities.

## Barriers to Reporting and Justice

Our research and community engagement work has identified critical barriers that prevent survivors from coming forward:

### Stigma and Social Shame
Survivors and their families often face stigmatization when abuse is revealed. In many contexts, the victim is blamed rather than the perpetrator, leading families to hide abuse to "preserve honor." This stigma silences survivors and protects abusers.

### Distrust of the Justice System
Many communities lack confidence in law enforcement and judicial systems. Corruption, lengthy court processes, and inadequate penalties mean that perpetrators often escape accountability. This institutional failure disempowers survivors and sends a message that abuse is tolerable.

### Poverty and Economic Vulnerability
Economic desperation leaves families and vulnerable children exposed to exploitation. Children in poverty face increased risk of sexual abuse, and economic dependence often prevents disclosure or seeking help. Families may prioritize survival over reporting abuse.

### Limited Access to Services
In rural and underserved areas, survivors often lack access to medical care, counseling, and legal assistance. The absence of safe spaces to report abuse or seek support forces survivors to suffer alone.

## INADESU's Response

Through our Health and Wellness department and Advocacy initiatives, INADESU addresses sexual violence through:

- **Community education** – Raising awareness about consent, healthy relationships, and safe reporting
- **School programs** – Teaching children about body safety and empowering them to speak up
- **Survivor support** – Connecting survivors with medical, psychological, and legal resources
- **Advocacy for policy change** – Working toward stronger protections and accountability for perpetrators

## What Communities Can Do

Breaking the cycle of sexual violence requires collective action:

1. **Listen without judgment** – Create safe spaces where survivors can disclose abuse
2. **Believe survivors** – The default response should be support, not suspicion
3. **Hold perpetrators accountable** – Don't protect abusers to preserve family or community honor
4. **Support protective systems** – Advocate for policies and programs that prevent abuse
5. **Teach children** – Help young people understand consent, safety, and healthy boundaries
